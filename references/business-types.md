# Business types: what buyers decide on

Read only the section that matches the offer. Each section covers what buyers in that category weigh, what must be on the page, and what most often kills the sale. If the offer mixes types (a course sold through an app, a product with a subscription), read both sections.

## Contents
- SaaS and apps (web and mobile)
- E-commerce product (own store)
- Marketplace listings (Amazon, Etsy, eBay, app stores, Shopify-style marketplaces)

## SaaS and apps (web and mobile)
**Usual goal:** free trial or free signup, then upgrade to paid.

**Buyers decide on:** whether it solves their specific problem, how fast they'll see value, how it compares with free or familiar tools, price versus what they use now, and how easy it is to leave.

**Must have:**
- A concrete outcome in the headline, not a category label.
- Real product visuals (screenshots, short demo) showing the result, not just the interface.
- Clear pricing with a recommended plan; free tier or trial terms stated (card or no card).
- Proof from users like the target buyer: testimonials with results, user counts, ratings.
- An answer to "why not [free or incumbent tool]?"
- Easy cancellation and data or privacy reassurance.

**Common killers:** headline describes features instead of outcomes; no reason to switch from what they already use; pricing hidden or confusing; free tier gives too little to show value, or so much that there's no reason to upgrade; the upgrade path after signup isn't visible on the page.

**Note:** for free-signup funnels, the page may convert to signups well while paid conversion suffers. Judge both: the page must earn the signup and also set up why someone will pay later.

## E-commerce product (own store)
**Usual goal:** add to cart and purchase.

**Buyers decide on:** what it looks and feels like, whether it'll work for them (size, fit, compatibility), total price including shipping, delivery time, return policy, and reviews from other buyers.

**Must have:**
- Multiple high-quality photos, including in-use or in-hand shots; video for products where motion matters.
- Specs that answer fit questions (dimensions, materials, sizing guidance, compatibility).
- Price, shipping cost, and delivery estimate visible before checkout.
- Reviews with ratings, ideally with photos, near the Add to Cart button.
- Return or exchange policy stated near the price.

**Common killers:** surprise shipping costs at checkout; few or poor photos; no reviews; unclear sizing; a return policy that's hidden or strict; the brand feels like an anonymous dropshipping store.

## Marketplace listings (Amazon, Etsy, eBay, app stores)
**Usual goal:** purchase or install, while competing side by side with similar listings.

**Buyers decide on:** the thumbnail and title in search results first, then price, rating and review count, photos, delivery speed, and how the listing compares with the ones next to it.

**Must have:**
- A main image that stands out in a grid of competitors and shows the product clearly.
- A title with the terms buyers search for, plus the key differentiator.
- Photos covering scale, details, in-use shots, and what's included.
- Bullet points or description that answer fit and quality questions quickly.
- A review strategy; low review counts are a major barrier.

**Common killers:** a thumbnail that looks like every other listing; price out of line with similar listings without a visible reason; few reviews; a description written for the seller rather than the buyer; slow shipping relative to competitors.

**Note:** the "alternatives" step matters most here, since competitors are literally on the same screen. Compare against the top 2–3 listings for the same search.

## Services and local businesses
**Usual goal:** call, book, request a quote, or visit.

**Buyers decide on:** whether they can trust this person or company, whether they serve this area and need, how much it will roughly cost, how fast they can get it done, and what other customers say.

**Must have:**
- Service area and specific services listed clearly.
- Real photos of the team, work, or location (not stock).
- Reviews (ideally linked to Google or Yelp), credentials, licenses, insurance where relevant.
- Pricing or price ranges, or at least how pricing works.
- A frictionless way to book or contact: phone number tappable on mobile, short form, online booking.
- Response time expectations.

**Common killers:** no prices at all; contact form as the only option; no reviews or proof of past work; unclear service area; a dated or broken-looking site that suggests the business is too.

## B2B and high-ticket offers
**Usual goal:** book a demo or call, request a proposal, or start a pilot.

**Buyers decide on:** business outcome and ROI, risk of a bad decision (their reputation is on the line), fit with existing tools and processes, security and compliance, and whether peers like them use it.

**Must have:**
- An outcome stated in business terms (time saved, revenue gained, cost cut, risk reduced).
- Case studies and logos from comparable companies; specific numbers.
- Clear explanation of how it works and how implementation goes.
- Security, compliance, and integration information.
- A low-commitment next step alongside the demo (pricing range, product tour, calculator, pilot).
- Material the champion can share internally to convince other decision-makers.

**Common killers:** vague "platform" language; no pricing signal at all; only one high-commitment call to action ("Book a demo"); no proof from similar companies; ignoring the other stakeholders (finance, IT, leadership) who must also say yes.

## Courses, coaching, and info products
**Usual goal:** purchase, enroll, apply, or book a discovery call.

**Buyers decide on:** whether the creator is credible, whether it will work for someone like them (skill level, time available), what exactly they'll get, whether they could learn it free elsewhere, and what happens if it doesn't work.

**Must have:**
- A specific transformation (from where to where, in how long).
- Who it's for and who it's not for.
- The creator's credibility: results, experience, story.
- Curriculum or what's included, made concrete (modules, hours, sessions, access length).
- Student results and testimonials with specifics.
- A guarantee, clearly stated.
- An honest answer to "why pay when there's free content?"

**Common killers:** vague promises; no proof of student results; unclear time commitment; hype that triggers skepticism; no guarantee; price with no anchor.

## Subscriptions and memberships (consumer)
**Usual goal:** start a subscription or trial.

**Buyers decide on:** ongoing value versus ongoing cost, how easy it is to pause or cancel, flexibility (skip, swap, change plan), and whether they'll actually use it.

**Must have:**
- What each delivery or month includes, shown concretely.
- Price per period and per unit, total commitment, and renewal terms in plain words.
- Easy cancel, pause, and skip stated prominently.
- Proof from long-term subscribers, not just first impressions.

**Common killers:** fear of being trapped; unclear renewal; value that's hard to picture beyond the first month; no way to try it before committing.
